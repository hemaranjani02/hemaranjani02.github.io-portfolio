Protecting student identity while measuring engagement in online classes

M.S. thesis, 2023 · Texas A&M University–Commerce · Advisor: Dr. Yuehua Wang

Computer VisionAI Privacy & SecurityGenerative Models (GANs)Adversarial MLAffective ComputingEdTech

Virtual classrooms often require students to keep their webcams on so instructors can gauge attention. Continuous video, however, exposes each student's facial identity to spoofing, misuse and identity theft. I developed a privacy-preserving framework that addresses both needs. It de-identifies student faces by combining adversarial cloaking (Fawkes) with GAN-based attribute editing (AttGAN), and it measures engagement from facial behavior using OpenFace features and a neural network classifier. Instructors can assess engagement without exposing who the student is.

"Bald" attribute. The hybrid beat both models on all 13 attributes tested, by about 12 points on average over AttGAN. Evaluated with 5,200 AWS face comparisons (4 models × 13 attributes × 100 samples) at an 80% match threshold, drawn from 20,599 held-out CelebA images.

92.1%

Test accuracy detecting student engagement, comparable with SVM, KNN, Random Forest and XGBoost baselines.

DAiSEE dataset: 7,348 videos, about 2.2 million frames. Trained on 5,482 videos and tested on 1,866 unseen videos.

Why it matters

  • Privacy and usefulness together. Encryption hides a face completely and makes engagement impossible to measure. My approach keeps the behavior signal while removing the identity.
  • Fairer by design. The engagement model learns from facial behavior instead of raw faces, so it judges what a student is doing, not who the student is.
  • Grounded in real attacks. Every protected face was tested against a commercial face recognition service, AWS Rekognition.
